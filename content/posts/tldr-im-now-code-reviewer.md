Thanks to Matt Pocock, I'm now a code reviewer. This is my own opinion, but I never liked AI and never had any interest in it. These days I use it on one condition.

The condition is simple. I refuse to let it work alone, and I have to be the Human in the Loop (HITL).

That condition didn't come out of nowhere. Programming on my own is draining. I take the requirements, explain everything to people who don't write code, build what they ask for, and then deal with the complaints. By the end my head hurts, but the user only sees the product ship without breaking, and nobody counts the headaches or the sleepless nights.

Meanwhile, I keep seeing vibecoders on social media bragging about what their AI built, and I'm not impressed at all. Yet I couldn't shake the feeling of falling behind. I used to think I didn't need any of it, that I could handle everything myself, until I asked myself what all of this was even for. The answer was just a low paycheck and daily exhaustion, and I wasn't sure I could keep up with this era. Somewhere along the way I gave my soul to harnesses, models, and agents.

So I started looking for a setup that fit my tight budget but still gave decent results. Some people treat AI like an AFK machine, and my wallet would cry if I copied them, so instead of burning tokens like that, I stay in the loop. It takes longer, sure, but at least I know what code my agents are writing.

Then I found Matt Pocock's skills, and they were exactly what I needed. There's a grilling session that pushes back on my biased prompts and turns them into specs and tickets, and when the agent implements the code, it runs the tests too. After that, the reviewing is on me, and I keep cycling until every feature is done.

So that's where I'm at, trying to treat AI like a coworker and still figuring it out as I go.
